Vesuvia

2017, rev. 2024

They say they found her body on our hotel room floor. They say that her fragile, typically sweet jasmine-scented neck was blackened with bruises, and that her hair—undeniably graying, despite her attempts at denial—was dampened on the left side of her head, just above her white-pearl pierced ear, with blood. Actually, I rescind—not dampened. Rather, soaked. They say that her temporal hairline, the crevice at which she secures her hair so as to keep it from her face, marks the location of her skull's deep fracture. They say it was split—her skull—from the temporal hairline to the crown of her head, indicating some sort of bludgeon to the back-left area. They say, though, that this blow didn't kill her—that it was delivered postmortem. They maintain that the cause of death was strangulation. They say that she asphyxiated—that her life was slowly stolen from her, even while she tried desperately to cling to it, as revealed by the residue of skin and blood beneath her fingertips. And they say I strangled her. They say I choked her with my own bare hands. They say she angered me beyond a tolerable point—ignited me like a flame, or spark, as if I were one of her cigarettes—and that I simply snapped…that my brain simply shattered into thousands of little fragments, scattered here and there just like the shards of glass from the Venetian vase they say they found in our hotel room, and which they say is now only a base, riddled with my palm and thumb prints. I shake, now—uncontrollably. My hands are clenched, and clench tighter and tighter, until my nails break through a first layer of skin, creating little patterned red marks that sting with anxious sweat. I look down now…my hands, moving themselves, turn upward. Palms up, towards the sky—or rather, towards the molded cream-shaded ceiling - and in my ears, there's this buzzing, gloating, ringing "no!" … "No, William!" Mother was right—the water was too strong. Claus had even warned us earlier, during the drive over. He said clearly, in that proper, over-eager little voice of his: "Miss, I'd advise staying out of the water today. Rough tide. Keep little Will on the sand." He shifted his gaze upward to the driver's mirror, and—in a new, higher pitch—said, "If it gets too hot, Willy, just dip your feet in. Nothing above the ankles! A small boy like you—it'll sweep you right away! Take you…take you right to Vesuvia!" And he said it with a teasing smile. He'd started saying that lately. As if it were his. Vesuvia was a bad place—one that mother always warned me I'd go if I didn't follow the rules. But I wanted to see what it looked like…to know if it smelled funny…if it was too hot, or too cold. Vesuvia…it was the grandest mystery of all. But it wasn't Claus's word. It was hers. "No! That's too far, William!" I heard her distant screams just over the tide's roar. It was too far, but it was also too late. I was suddenly swept up from underneath, and my feet flung into the air forcing me first on my back, and then under the water. I was tugged and pulled—like a new, fought-over school toy—in every direction, with no control, no dictation, of my own. I swallowed salty water and sand, and, for a moment, could swear I was choking. I couldn't breathe…I couldn't see, but also couldn't force my eyes shut. And all I could feel were the grains of sand collecting in my bottoms. I was swirling around, and around…and then it stopped. I broke through the surface, first my head, then my arms. The first thing I wanted was air—so I breathed in deeply, as if never having tasted it before. And the second thing I craved was mother. So I ran, as fast as I could—despite the water's relentless pulling—towards her. "William," she exhaled, through sighs of relief, "never do that again." She pulled me in, and held me tightly so that the side of my head was pressed against her chest, gently crushed beneath her grip. "Thank goodness. Thank goodness you're alright." … They make me remove my clothes. First to go is my white button-down which smells of Dom Perignon and which is stained with spatters of blood. Then shoes, then pants. Now I am utterly bare…stripped…and I feel them laughing at me. Eyes… from behind, watching me, gawking at me—as if I'm a monster. I walk, hesitantly, to the white wall. "Hands," he demands. I hold them out, open them up, and immediately the camera flashes. The light pains me. I squint my eyes in confusion, stepping back a bit. My brain…my brain feels heavy, but not in the feverish sort of way. I get the strange sensation that it's been flipped, that it's been somehow turned entirely up-side-down. I oddly feel that my head is too large for my body—immensely too large—and that it is pounding in a painful rhythm. Rhythmic chaos. The officer turns my hands over now, downward, so that the tops of my wrists and forearms reveal scratches and dry blood to the camera. Another flash. And then more. I am instructed to turn around, to face the white wall and expose my backside. I oblige. Now— "Come this way." I follow, again hesitantly, and cover my exposed lower area with both hands. I feel like a little boy—like a scared, pathetic little boy. A gutless coward—not a man. I think I want to cry. "Here," he says, and tosses a blatantly too-large, pale orange jumpsuit at me. "That'll suit you, right?" He smirks. "The great and glorious Bourdillons." He pronounces my last name slowly, mockingly emphasizing each of its syllables and attempting his best French. "Yea, fucking right. You're fucked up…all of you." I put the pants on first, still trying to cover myself with one hand, and so beginning this sort of loser's dance—pulling up, covering, and half-hopping all at once, like an old, injured bird. He watches me. "Sick fuck." … I was clean, and I smelled of fresh milk, black charcoal, and honey. The steam from the shower still fogged the mirror, and I wiped it away with my hand so as to see myself while brushing my teeth. I closed my eyes, and breathed in—the combination of steam, mint, milk, charcoal and honey successfully soothed me. I'd always done this routine during a fight. The shower's glass walls made me deaf to the noise. They blocked out the deep, mean shouts and the occasional mellowed shrieks. I think I hated him. I hated his deep, thick voice…the roars his car's engine would make late at night, signifying either his arrival or departure…his veiny hands…the marks they'd so often leave on mother. But he was so big…he was so tall, so angry. And he made me feel small. Admittedly, I was growing—when I looked in them, mirrors now revealed new definition in my abdomen and arm muscles, and some faint hairs in my armpits and elsewhere. When I showed mother, she told me I was becoming a man. Maybe, someday soon, I could protect her. There was a quiet knocking on my door, and—knowing it was her—I ran from the mirror to open it. "William! I've brought you your tea. It's still warm, have it soon before it cools…Oh, your bed isn't made for nighttime. You should sleep soon! It's late…Actually, it's quite late. Why are you still awake? You shouldn't have stayed up…Your curtains, they aren't even closed…" I interrupted her rambles, as I recently began to do when she was shaken, or nervous. She would've continued on forever if I hadn't. "Mother, I've waited up for you. I knew you'd come soon." She smiled at this, now, and pulled me close to her, meshing our scents—hers of soft jasmine and cold sweat—together, and somewhat drying my still-wet hair against her shirt. I stood there like that, against her, for a while. I liked the way her dancing fingers felt on my scalp. But suddenly I felt strange. I loosened her embrace and stepped out of it. Something was different—something was off. I became overwhelmingly and utterly aware of my nakedness. No. "Mother, stop." I stepped backwards, away from her, fully exposing myself and realizing it. I lowered my hands instinctually, to cover myself, and looked up at her, revealing a set of bewildered, pathetic eyes—a child's eyes. She shouldn't see me like this. But it's only mother…what could possibly be the matter? "What's wrong, William? You look afraid—why? It's alright, everything is alright." She smiled, just slightly. I nodded at her reassurance. She stepped towards me now, took my hand, brought it to her lips, and kissed the top of it. "You're everything in the world to me, William. You are everything, my sweet William." I believed her, but my cheeks burned. And inside my head, the shrieking was louder than it had ever been before—no. … They lead me to a small room now, supposedly for further, more intensive, questioning. "William Bourdillon," the man sitting across the table says, as he stops tapping his pencil, tucks it behind his ear, and leans back in his chair. "What to do now?" I feel paralyzed…even while sitting upright. I feel that some sort of demonic creature, perhaps, was crouched on my chest. "What to make of this, mishap, shall we call it?" The demon pushes on my chest, now, slowly compressing my wildly pounding heart, threatening to crack my rib bones one by one. "Is that what you'd call it, William? A mishap? La mésaventure?" I swallow dry air. I can't answer—I don't know. … No one answered Mr. Rousell's questions today, but regardless, he continued to ask them. He didn't show it, but I knew he pitied himself. This was all he amounted to…a worn-out, bland, almost certainly unmarried Kingsworth schoolteacher, who desperately sought prestige and taught ancient and useless literature to fifteen-year-old boys that would only forget his "wisdom" and inevitably still surpass him. "Would anyone care to read, then? Lines 1420-1440. Anyone?" He was pleading now. Silence. I couldn't help but smirk—the poor man was entirely spineless. He looked around frantically, now, and I made an utter, intractable mistake—I looked upwards instead of to the right. Upwards—the genius that I was—and our eyes met, even if just for the faintest instant. "William! Please, would you begin? Lines 1420-1440, please, just where we left off—you should have it bookmarked. Go on." And he motioned, too excitedly, with head and hands, towards the copy he was holding. I was trapped. I rolled my eyes, pointedly, and cleared my throat— "SECOND MESSENGER: Shortest to hear and tell—our glorious queen Jocasta's dead. CHORUS: Unhappy woman! How? SECOND MESSENGER: By her own hand. The worst of what was done you cannot know. You did not see the sight. Yet in so far as I remember it you'll hear the end of our unlucky queen. When she came raging into the house she went straight to her marriage bed, tearing her hair with both her hands, and crying upon Laius long dead—Do you remember, Laius, that night long past which bred a child for us to send you to your death and leave a mother making children with her son? And then she groaned and cursed the bed in which she brought forth husband by her husband, children by her own child, an infamous double bond. How after that she died I do not know, —for Oedipus distracted us from seeing." I returned to the room as if I'd left. "Excellent William! Excellently done! A fine job indeed." I sat up straighter just then, and felt, in an odd sort of way, different. Words…words from without…words…daggers, within. By her own hand? "And William, after reading it with such vigor—after breathing such life into it—please share with us…what do you make of this terrible tragedy?" Infamous double bond…is it so? I couldn't answer—I didn't know. I knew nothing other than that these words were perhaps the most logical ones I'd ever heard. And they cut me deeply…made me bleed, but heeled me…carved me up into bits, yet made me feel more whole than I'd ever felt…exposed me, and veiled me. I swallowed. She was before me, now—my own Queen, my Jocasta—hanging, neck in noose, with eyes that were writhing and rolling back in guilt, and with lips that were turning the faintest, most beautiful blue. "Rhythmic chaos." It was all I could manage. … "Tell me if I'm getting it all correct, William." And he restates his story again, objectively and emptily, from his choice of starting point, as if he were with me—as if he had been there, been physically present—all along. And he speaks with such conviction. "You decide to visit some friends for the weekend here, in California—friends from Kingsworth—whom you hadn't seen since they moved away from your place of mutual residence in Neuilly three years ago. Your mother comes along, simply because she'd prefer it to staying alone. So, the two of you find a hotel, Fairmont Grand Del Mar, and you plan to stay there together, in your suite, for three nights." I nod, mindlessly, in agreement. He is correct. "However, on the second night, just some 17 hours ago, as you embark on another evening of reckless debauchery, leaving your mother behind to sleep, I suppose, you encounter an interesting…hm…an exciting offer. Your friend, Benjamin, asks you to join him in purchasing 1200 micrograms of lysergic acid diethylamide, or, as you plainly refer to it, acid. You agree. Is that correct?" I blink. I think I am nodding. "Yes or no? I need an answer, William." "Yes," I swallow, and clutch onto the metal-slab table to hold myself upright. "Good. And later in the evening, you, Benjamin, Pierre, and Jonathon divide up the acid strips among yourselves. You also take about 1 gram of cocaine, by nasal inhalation." Pounding, again. The room is pulsing in and out, chewing and then digesting me. My brain is floating… churning in its blood bath, creating ripples, then waves. Blood oozes out from my ears, and then from my nostrils, onto my lips so that I taste it…forces its way back into me…then spills out of the corners of both my eyes, piercing them and blinding me…covering my face in wet blood and vitreous. Blink hard. "What happens next, William?" My lips can't make even the faintest sound…drying blood seals them shut. The door opens; another man enters the room. He sits, tosses what looks like an envelope onto the table. "I'll tell you what fucking happens next. This thing," he points to me now, "returns to his hotel room 6 hours later, at around 3:30 am, and somehow, over the span of the next half-hour or so, brutally strangles his mother to death—breaking her neck in the process. He then bludgeons her skull with the base of a broken bedside vase, splitting it from the temporal hairline to the crown of the head. And what next? He falls asleep." "I'm going to ask you again, William—why?" … I walked, slowly and hazily up our spiraled staircase, gripping onto the side railing with one hand, and pushing tufts of hair out of my face with the other. The celebration was still going on—and would continue until around three or four in the morning, I knew, despite his having already left with a woman. Bourdillon Christmas was never a disappointment. But mother was nowhere to be found. I had been watching her, earlier—I had sat with her as she got ready for the evening. She wore a rose red gown, that trailed a bit in the back, as it was tailored to, and she'd had her hair done up, secured on the top with a diamond-engraved headpiece. I liked the way it caught the light each time she spun or twirled. I arrived at her door, now, gently forced it open, and entered her and my father's bedroom. She was a heap of red, sprawled on top of white linens, one arm dangling over the edge of the bed, hand open, just above a small bottle and a dozen or so scattered pill capsules. For a moment, an odd sense of serenity took me. Her arm was limp and rotting, she was sinking, elegantly, into a pool of her own blood, and she was a red stain on fresh, white linen. She was a bloodied knife-wound on flesh. And then she returned. "William." Her head lifted, just barely, and she turned towards me. The visible slits of her eyes were entirely glossed over…they were shining, so lucidly, in a strange, beautiful way. "How much did you take tonight, Mother? It needs to stop…this needs to end." But I couldn't say anymore. I walked over towards the bed, and sat on the corner of it. "William," she made out, again, this time entirely inaudibly—but I could read her lips. "Come. Come with me, darling…into our own world, where he is not…our own, special place…a secret space neither here nor there…not anywhere." She lifted her limp hand, and with it, softly touched the back of my neck, bringing me towards her. Our own, special place. … They continue to tell their story, but I am no longer present. My eyes close, tiredly. I try…I try to go back, to recount. Start with this morning, a few eternities back… I wake and feel inexorably nauseated. Find my way, crawling, to the bathroom, and spill out my insides. My head is a stone. Panic—I know that my heart is beating too fast. And it won't stop. On the outside I'm paralyzed. But inside, my heart, a prisoner, pounds wildly against its cage…raging, belligerent and mad. Stand, clutch the sink countertop. Lift my head…wait until the rush ends…until the black fog drifts to my peripherals, bordering, rather than stealing, my vision. I am white—whiter than I've ever been. A ghost, surely. Dead…rotting somewhere…dust and dirt and bones and stench. Something is behind me, made visible by the mirror…lying on the ground. Turn. And then—darkness. … My tongue was numb, and my fingertips were tingling. Bright lights were everywhere, engulfing me…they shined and spun and spattered, all to the beat—louder was brighter, quieter and they flickered like flames, no sound and they were gone, darkness. He spoke and I heard chartreuse. She whispered and it sounded of sweet lavender. Sweet lavender. My eyelids separated…eyes opened to a new world. Sounds of music had stopped. New place, new walls, no light. Darkness in a bright hallway. I sat on a boat that was floating in place on a purple-water stream. Stood up and stepped out, foot breaking water's surface…the apex, the center. Concentric ripples vibrated through a red and gold carpet. A chamber door awaited me, and I blew it open with a puff. It opened as a mouth would, with teeth that separated as jaw widened, revealing a light pink, wet tongue that extended forward, marking my pathway. I walked in, feet on tongue. And then I heard it—flowing blood…gushing and gurgling, and shrieking loudly, stabbing each of my ears and piercing my vision. I'd heard it before…the scratching. I wanted it to stop. I knew that I could make it stop. I saw the creature of the shrieking before me…her familiar smell burned holes through my chest. No. Then she danced in the air above, fingers conducting a symphony, feet dangling, kicking one way and then the other—a cabaret dancer performing the cancan. She hung by a blue vein tied thrice around her neck, and I knew the vein was her own. Must have gotten lose. We could dance if I quieted her. Squeeze the source of sound. I emptied out every last blood red drop. Smash the jukebox in. I swallowed the sound, tasted the symphony, breathed in the soft melody. Sweet to the tongue. … Blinking, I force myself to return to the small room of cracked walls. "A monster! A bestial monster! You…you can pay for lawyers to conjure up an intricate story of your innocence—blame it, undeniably, on the intoxication, on whatever you'd like." His voice is boiling…becoming louder, and louder. Why? … Exhale. Eyes close, then open—open to the same, new world. Filth. Stench. Four white walls…no window, no light. Here I am, now…I'm where I'll be forever…where I was created, and where, surely, I was fated to return. Here, in my own, special place: Vesuvia. Mother must be smiling.
